# How we chose the categories

This is the category breakdown. Eight areas. The readiness topics under each one are the things the hospital has to be on top of. The evidence line is the 2015 Joint Commission standard that defines that topic.

The `x / 5` line is blank on purpose. A score is a judgment of current operations, and it can be assigned only after evidence is attached. These workbooks are the standard the hospital is measured against. They are not a review of MLKCH's operations, so there is no honest `3 / 5` yet. When a feed exists, the same card gets a score using the rubric in [03-Compliance-Scorecard.md](03-Compliance-Scorecard.md).

The long tables behind each card (every standard, EP counts, what a surveyor is shown) are in [03-Compliance-Scorecard.md](03-Compliance-Scorecard.md). This file is the map.

## 01 People and competence

3 readiness areas. Can the hospital show that the people doing the work are qualified, trained, and currently competent, and that nursing leadership and the medical staff are properly constituted.

### Staff qualifications and competence
— / 5 · Not assessed

The hospital has to define the staff it needs, verify licenses and education, orient people, keep training going, assess competence for the job they actually do, and evaluate performance on a cycle. This is the file a surveyor pulls after a tracer, not a policy on a shelf.

Evidence · HR.01.01.01 · HR.01.02.01 · HR.01.02.05 · HR.01.02.07 · HR.01.04.01 · HR.01.05.03 · HR.01.06.01 · HR.01.07.01

### Nursing leadership and staffing plan
— / 5 · Not assessed

A nurse executive, qualified for the role, directs nursing services, sets the guidelines, and owns the nurse staffing plan. This area is the leadership structure. Bedside practice sits under patient safety and under care delivery, which is not in this packet.

Evidence · NR.01.01.01 · NR.01.02.01 · NR.02.01.01 · NR.02.02.01 · NR.02.03.01

### Medical staff credentialing and privileging
— / 5 · Not assessed

The medical staff chapter is not in this folder. Surveyors still spend a dedicated session on appointment, reappointment, and privileges, and the federal requirement is an organized medical staff under 42 CFR 482.22. Treat this card as in the model and unscored, not as a pass.

Evidence · MS chapter absent from the packet · survey activity "Medical Staff Credentialing & Privileging"

## 02 Medication system

2 readiness areas. How medications are chosen, stored, ordered, checked, dispensed, and given.

### Medication control
— / 5 · Not assessed

The hospital has to plan medication management, keep a hospital-specific high-alert and hazardous list, manage look-alike/sound-alike drugs, select and procure from a formulary, store securely (including temperature and expiration), control emergency medications, patient-brought medications, recalls, and returns.

Evidence · MM.01.01.01 · MM.01.01.03 · MM.01.02.01 · MM.02.01.01 · MM.03.01.01 · MM.03.01.03 · MM.03.01.05 · MM.05.01.17 · MM.05.01.19

### Medication use process
— / 5 · Not assessed

Orders have to be complete, a pharmacist has to review them, preparation and labeling have to be safe, dispensing and after-hours access have to be controlled, administration has to follow the process, and adverse drug events have to be caught and reviewed. This is the part a tracer watches on the unit.

Evidence · MM.04.01.01 · MM.05.01.01 · MM.05.01.07 · MM.05.01.09 · MM.05.01.11 · MM.05.01.13 · MM.06.01.01 · MM.06.01.03 · MM.06.01.05 · MM.07.01.03 · MM.08.01.01

## 03 Patient safety practices

2 readiness areas. The National Patient Safety Goals and the Universal Protocol. These are practices surveyors sample. They are not satisfied by a policy alone.

### Identification, medications, alarms, and handoffs of results
— / 5 · Not assessed

Two patient identifiers, transfusion matching, timely critical results, labeling on the sterile field, anticoagulant safety, medication reconciliation, and clinical alarm safety.

Evidence · NPSG.01.01.01 · NPSG.01.03.01 · NPSG.02.03.01 · NPSG.03.04.01 · NPSG.03.05.01 · NPSG.03.06.01 · NPSG.06.01.01

### Infection practices, suicide risk, and procedures
— / 5 · Not assessed

Hand hygiene, multidrug-resistant organisms, central lines, surgical sites, urinary catheters, suicide risk screening, and the three Universal Protocol steps before a procedure: verify, mark the site, time-out. The workbook cell for the verification standard literally says `MORE TEXT HIDDEN IN ROW`; the requirement is still in the model via the rest of the Universal Protocol block.

Evidence · NPSG.07.01.01 · NPSG.07.03.01 · NPSG.07.04.01 · NPSG.07.05.01 · NPSG.07.06.01 · NPSG.15.01.01 · UP.01.01.01 · UP.01.02.01 · UP.01.03.01

## 04 Infection, testing, and tissue

3 readiness areas.

### Infection prevention program
— / 5 · Not assessed

A named owner, resources, a risk assessment, goals, a plan, a way to handle an influx of infectious patients, day-to-day implementation, device and supply reprocessing, transmission precautions, an offer of influenza vaccination to staff and practitioners, and a review of whether the plan worked.

Evidence · IC.01.01.01 · IC.01.01.02 · IC.01.03.01 · IC.01.04.01 · IC.01.05.01 · IC.01.06.01 · IC.02.01.01 · IC.02.02.01 · IC.02.03.01 · IC.02.04.01 · IC.03.01.01

### Waived testing
— / 5 · Not assessed

Whoever is named on the CLIA certificate owns the waived tests. Policies exist, the people who run the tests are competent, quality control is done on each procedure, and the records are kept. This is glucometers and similar tests, not the full clinical laboratory. A moderate- or high-complexity lab is outside this packet.

Evidence · WT.01.01.01 · WT.02.01.01 · WT.03.01.01 · WT.04.01.01 · WT.05.01.01

### Tissue and organ responsibilities
— / 5 · Not assessed

Agreements with the organ procurement organization and tissue banks, standardized tissue handling, the ability to trace tissue both directions, and investigation of adverse events. For a community hospital this is often the agreement and the process when a death might be a donor, plus tissue implants if they do orthopedic or wound procedures. Whether they implant tissue is still an open question, and it changes how large this card is.

Evidence · TS.01.01.01 · TS.02.01.01 · TS.03.01.01 · TS.03.02.01 · TS.03.03.01

## 05 Record and rights

2 readiness areas. The chart, and the patient's rights around that care.

### Medical record
— / 5 · Not assessed

The record has defined contents, entries are authenticated, documentation is timely, the hospital audits its own records, retention is defined, operative and sedation care is documented, verbal orders are recorded by qualified staff, and discharge information is in the record. This is the highest process-sampling chapter in the packet: surveyors open charts.

Evidence · RC.01.01.01 · RC.01.02.01 · RC.01.03.01 · RC.01.04.01 · RC.01.05.01 · RC.02.01.01 · RC.02.01.03 · RC.02.01.07 · RC.02.03.07 · RC.02.04.01

### Patient rights
— / 5 · Not assessed

Rights and dignity, information the patient can understand, informed consent, recordings, research protections, end-of-life decisions, freedom from abuse and neglect, and a real complaint and grievance process. Several standards apply only if the hospital has swing beds, a long-term psychiatric service, or a primary care medical home election. Those should be marked not applicable once someone confirms the service list, not scored as missing.

Evidence · RI.01.01.01 · RI.01.01.03 · RI.01.02.01 · RI.01.03.01 · RI.01.03.03 · RI.01.03.05 · RI.01.05.01 · RI.01.06.03 · RI.01.07.01 · RI.02.01.01

## 06 Care delivery

1 readiness area. This is the hole in the packet, and it is what tracers spend most of their time on.

### Provision of care, treatment, and services
— / 5 · Not assessed

The chapter is listed on the index and the file is not here. It is assessment, the plan of care, orders carried out, restraint and seclusion, operative and anesthesia care, and coordination through discharge. Pieces show up elsewhere (restraint policy is on the survey document list, patient flow is under leadership, operative documentation is under the medical record, nursing leadership is under people). The chapter itself should stay on the scorecard as not in the source packet.

Evidence · PC chapter absent from the packet

## 07 Leadership and improvement

### Leadership and governance
— / 5 · Not assessed

Governing body accountability, a chief executive, an organized medical staff accountable to the board, culture of safety, conflict of interest, budget, department management, contracted services, patient flow, and an organization-wide patient safety program. Most of these are structural: minutes, bylaws, and policies, not a sample of patients.

— / 5 · Not assessed

The hospital collects data, analyzes it, improves, and can show performance on its accountability measures and on staffing-effectiveness indicators. Surveyors ask for twelve months of this on the morning they arrive.

## 08 Place, utilities, emergencies, and information

4 readiness areas. The building, the equipment, the disaster plan, and the duty to protect information.

### Life safety
— / 5 · Not assessed

The building meets the Life Safety Code: egress, fire and smoke barriers, alarms, extinguishing systems, and protection of occupants while construction is underway. A life safety surveyor walks this on every hospital survey. `LS.02` and `LS.03` repeat the themes for different occupancy types; a room should be scored under the occupancy it actually is.

Evidence · LS.01.01.01 · LS.01.02.01 · LS.02.01.20 · LS.02.01.30 · LS.02.01.34 · LS.02.01.35 · LS.03.01.20

### Environment of care
— / 5 · Not assessed

Safety and security, hazardous materials, fire drills, inspection of fire safety equipment, medical equipment inventory and maintenance, utilities, emergency power, medical gas, safe space during construction, and a yearly look at whether those plans worked.

Evidence · EC.02.01.01 · EC.02.02.01 · EC.02.03.03 · EC.02.03.05 · EC.02.04.01 · EC.02.04.03 · EC.02.05.01 · EC.02.05.07 · EC.04.01.01

### Emergency management
— / 5 · Not assessed

A hazard vulnerability analysis, an emergency operations plan, and prepared approaches for communications, resources, security, staff, utilities, and patients, including disaster privileges for volunteer practitioners, plus exercises and an evaluation. The file is mislabeled "Emergency Medicine." The standards are emergency management.

Evidence · EM.01.01.01 · EM.02.01.01 · EM.02.02.01 · EM.02.02.13 · EM.03.01.03

### Information privacy, security, and continuity
— / 5 · Not assessed

The hospital plans how information is managed, can continue during downtime, protects privacy, keeps health information secure and intact, and can retrieve it in a useful form. This is also the list of duties a product takes on if it stores health information.

Evidence · IM.01.01.01 · IM.01.01.03 · IM.02.01.01 · IM.02.01.03 · IM.02.02.03

## How a card gets a number later

These labels apply to evidence that has been ingested, not to these spreadsheets:

| Mean of the area's requirements | Label |
|---|---|
| under 1.5 | Material gap |
| 1.5 to under 3 | Partially ready |
| 3 to under 4.2 | Largely ready |
| 4.2 to 5 | Ready |

Each requirement underneath is 0 to 5: nothing on file, owner only, current policy, a sample inside the required window, that sample pulled from a named system, and for process requirements a rate that is actually trending. Weights and the exposure index (which chapter a survey presses hardest) stay in [03-Compliance-Scorecard.md](03-Compliance-Scorecard.md). Exposure is not a substitute for the score on these cards.
