# How a hospital accreditation survey actually works

This is the process behind the workbooks. The workbooks are the 2015 rule list. The survey is how someone checks the rule list in the building.

Two clocks are easy to mix up. The **2015 packet** still uses the old scoring marks (`A`, `C`, documentation required, measure of success). The **survey process described by Joint Commission now** displays findings on the SAFER matrix and asks for corrective evidence within 60 days. Both are real. They are not the same era. This pass does not rewrite the 2015 EPs into the 2026 manual.

Confidence is marked per section. Citations are in [07-Sources.md](07-Sources.md).

## Who is auditing, and why it feels like more than one audit

MLKCH opened in 2015 after California licensing and Joint Commission accreditation. Confirmed, MLKCH's own opening announcement.

For a hospital that uses Joint Commission for **deemed status**, the Joint Commission survey stands in for the Medicare survey of the Conditions of Participation. CMS still owns Medicare certification. The state (California Department of Public Health) still licenses the hospital under Title 22. The workbooks were built so one row can point at all three: the Joint Commission EP, a CMS citation, and a Title 22 flag.

That is why the same week of work gets described as "the audit." Staff are often preparing one binder that has to satisfy three readers. Confirmed as the structure of deemed-status accreditation and of these spreadsheets. The degree of overlap on any single future visit is Open.

## The cycle

Confirmed from Joint Commission's accreditation process page (current site, reviewed 22 September 2026):

- Most surveys are **unannounced**. Initial surveys that are not for deemed status are an exception. MLKCH's first survey in 2015 was an opening event; later surveys follow the unannounced rule.
- The expected window for the next full survey is **30 to 36 months** after the previous full survey (laboratories are on a shorter clock; that is a different program).
- Some organizations get seven days' notice for practical reasons. That is not the default, and it is not something to plan a product around.
- Surveyors may do early, late, night, or weekend activity after the survey has started. They do not do that before the opening conference. Confirmed, 2026 Hospital Accreditation Survey Process Guide.

MLKCH was surveyed recently. If that event was a full survey, the next unannounced window is likely measured in years, not weeks. The date itself is Open. Intracycle work still exists between full surveys. In the 2015 packet that work is the Focused Standards Assessment (`APR.03.01.01`, 7 EPs). Joint Commission has changed the between-survey program since 2015. The 2015 FSA form should not be assumed to be what they file today.

## How long they are in the building

Joint Commission sets length and team size from the Electronic Application for Accreditation (size and services), not from a public table of "131 beds = N days." Confirmed, Survey Process Guide.

Confirmed team rules:

- At least one nurse surveyor.
- A Life Safety Code surveyor on every hospital survey.
- On a team, one surveyor is the team leader and the hospital's point of contact.

A separate public sample agenda (not the 2025 activity guide) shows **three surveyors over five days** and says agendas vary. That is an example, not MLKCH. Confidence: Confirmed as an example; Open for this hospital.

Surveyors arrive about 7:45 a.m., identify themselves at the desk, and the hospital must confirm the visit on Joint Commission Connect before work proceeds. Someone with extranet credentials has to be reachable that morning. Confirmed, 2025 Survey Activity Guide.

## What they do on site

The 2025 Survey Activity Guide gives durations for activities, not a promise that every hospital gets every block at that length.

| Activity | Published duration | What it consumes |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival and preliminary planning, including a short safety briefing | 30–60 min | A base room with phone, power, internet; the document list starts immediately |
| Opening conference and orientation | 30–60 min | Leaders |
| Individual tracer | 60–120 min, every day, multiple patients | A clinician escort, the chart, the unit, interviews with whoever touched that patient |
| Competence assessment | 30–60 min | HR / educators, personnel files |
| Medical staff credentialing and privileging | 60 min | Medical staff office files. **The MS workbook is not in this folder.** |
| Emergency management | 60 min | Plans, hazard analysis, exercise records |
| Quality and performance improvement | 60–90 min | 12 months of data and project write-ups |
| Leadership | 60 min | Governing body, CEO, medical staff leadership |
| Life safety document review | 60 min plus a 120–150 min facility orientation block in the guide's two figures | The LS/EC document tool |
| Life safety building assessment | 2–5 hours per day | Facilities staff walking the building |
| Report preparation | 60–120 min | Surveyors, last day |
| CEO exit briefing | 15–30 min | CEO |
| Organization exit conference | 30–45 min | Broader leadership |

The guide also says surveyors may ask for more documents than the list, throughout the survey, to check what they saw or heard.

### The morning document list

The 2025 guide's requested-documentation list is the clearest published picture of the manual pile. It is current Joint Commission material, so some item names (ORYX / Accelerate PI) are newer than the 2015 packet. The habit is the same: produce it when they walk in. The list includes, among other items:

- License, CLIA certificates, org chart, map, list of all surveyable sites
- A **patient list with name, location, age, diagnosis, length of stay, admit date, and admission source**, plus the surgery and procedure schedules. This is PHI, handed over on paper or a screen in the base room so surveyors can pick tracers.
- Contract list, blood supplier agreement, organ and tissue procurement agreements
- 12 months of performance-improvement data, governing body minutes, medical executive committee minutes, environment-of-care minutes
- Emergency management documents (program, hazard vulnerability analysis, operations plan, exercises), described in the guide as reviewed at least every two years
- 12 months of infection surveillance, the annual infection risk assessment
- Medical staff bylaws, and any sections changed since the last full survey flagged
- Policies they name specifically: autopsy, blood transfusion, complaints, restraint and seclusion, waived testing, medication management, abuse and neglect, falls, antibiotic stewardship
- Sterile-compounding engineering-control reports, where that service exists
- Culture-of-safety evaluation data

The Life Safety and Environment of Care Document List and Review Tool (effective date printed as 3/1/2024) is a second stack: management plans, annual evaluations, fire drills, inspection and testing records for alarms, sprinklers, emergency power, medical gas, and medical equipment. The life safety surveyor starts that review soon after arrival.

This is why preparation runs for weeks even though the surveyors are on site for days. The surveyor does not need the hospital to write the policies during the survey. The hospital has to **find, date, and put in one room** a year of minutes, logs, and attestations, and have the right people free to walk and talk. If the logs live in spreadsheets on different shares, that assembly is manual even when the underlying work was done.

### Tracers are the part that pulls the floor

Confirmed, Joint Commission process page: surveyors use the hospital's own information to follow a patient's care across departments. They look for breaks inside a step or between departments (order to pharmacy to administration to documentation, for example).

A tracer is not a checklist meeting. It is a patient already in a bed. The surveyor reads the chart, goes to the unit, asks the nurse what the policy says, looks at the medication area, and may follow the same patient into imaging or the lab. Category `C` EPs in the 2015 packet are the ones designed to be judged by how often the process fails in a sample, not by whether a policy exists. Those are the EPs a tracer is built to catch.

Record of care is 75% category `C`. Life safety is 70%. Human resources is 55%. National Patient Safety Goals are 46%. Medication management is 39%. Leadership is 3% and nursing structure is 0%. The chapters that fail on the floor are not the same chapters that fail because a policy PDF is missing.

## How a failure is scored now

Confirmed, Joint Commission SAFER page and the 2022 issue of *The Source*:

- An EP that is out of compliance becomes a **Requirement for Improvement (RFI)**.
- Each RFI is plotted on the **SAFER matrix** by likelihood of harm (low, moderate, high) and scope (limited, pattern, widespread).
- The matrix is how Joint Commission communicates risk. The Survey Process Guide says placement on the matrix does not itself decide accreditation.
- If the finding maps to a Medicare **condition-level** deficiency, the follow-up is stricter than a standard-level finding. *The Source* (2022) describes condition-level items as needing to be corrected on a **45-day** clock, with the ESC report still inside the 60-day submission window. Read that article for the worked example; do not generalize one example to every CMS tag.
- **Immediate Threat to Life** is a separate, faster path. The 2015 packet names it in `APR.09.04.01`. Current operational deadlines for an immediate threat should be taken from the accreditation manual chapter in force at the time of the survey, not from memory. This pass did not rely on a specific hour count.

### What the hospital must send back

Confirmed, accreditation process page and *The Source* (2022):

- Final report posted on Joint Commission Connect, described as within **10 business days** after the survey.
- **Evidence of Standards Compliance (ESC)** due within **60 days**.
- The ESC has to say what was done, the date, who is accountable, and how compliance will be kept.
- Higher-risk SAFER cells also require a description of leadership involvement and a preventive analysis.
- The hospital can try to clarify or remove an RFI. Findings that exist because **required documentation was not available at the time of survey** have historically been hard to clarify away. A 2016 Joint Commission conference update said required-documentation findings were not eligible for clarification. That slide is 2016. Treat the current clarification rules as something to confirm in the manual for the survey year. Confidence: Confirmed for the 2016 statement; Open as a 2026 rule quote.

The 2015 workbooks have a `MOS` column (`M` on 263 EPs in this packet). In that era, a measure of success was a numeric audit showing the fix held, and category `C` findings were the ones that triggered it. Joint Commission later told customers the separate MOS process was a source of dissatisfaction and changed the model toward SAFER and a single 60-day ESC. Confidence: Confirmed as the 2016 direction of travel (HESNI conference slides) and as the current 60-day ESC rule. Whether any MOS-style data attachment still appears inside an ESC is a detail of the current manual. Open at that level of procedure.

## What the 2015 columns mean

These definitions match how Joint Commission labeled EPs in the manual era these spreadsheets were built from. Confidence: Confirmed against Joint Commission tagging language in the 2016 conference update and against the icons described in Joint Commission crosswalk material. The column **headers in the workbooks** match. The **integer `CR` column does not** match a published Joint Commission field.

| Workbook column | Values found | Meaning used in this project |
|---|---|---|
| `SC` | `A` 899, `C` 440, blank 4 (of 1,343) | `A` = structural (a policy, plan, or role exists). `C` = process, scored on how often it fails in a sample. |
| `DOC` | `D` 246, blank 1,095, `C` 2 | `D` = documentation must be available. The two `C` values sit in Medication Management and look like a column slip. |
| `MOS` | `M` 263, blank 1,080 | In the 2015 model, a numeric sustained measure is required if that EP is cited. |
| `ESP` | labels such as `ESP-1` | Present on the layout that has 11 columns. It does not reliably equal the EP number (Environment of Care rows show `ESP-1` on EPs 1, 2, and 3). Meaning inside MLKCH's sheet is Open. It was not used as a weight. |
| `CR` | blank 1,073, `3` 258, `2` 11, `1` 1 | Small integers. No Joint Commission definition found that says CR=3 means a specific risk tier. **Not used as if it were SAFER.** Open question for whoever built the crosswalk. |
| `CMS` | non-empty on 703 EPs | A crosswalk note. Often a section such as a Conditions of Participation cite. Not independently validated cell by cell. |
| `Title 22` | non-empty on 140 EPs | Often the word `Yes`, not a section number. Means "a state requirement was thought to apply." |
| `MLKCH Validation` | empty | No compliance state. |
| `Person Responsible` | empty except one cell containing `1` | No owners. |

Category `A` fails when the document or structure is missing. Category `C` can fail while the policy is perfect, because the sample of patients or staff did not match the policy. A product that only stores policies will light up the `D` and `A` rows and will stay blind on the `C` rows. Those `C` rows are where record of care, life safety, competency, and medication administration actually get cited.

## Where the manual work sits

| Stage | What is manual | What is already digital somewhere | Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Between surveys | Mock tracers, chasing signatures, rebuilding the document index, assigning owners. The blank owner column is this stage. | Policies may be in a policy system or a shared drive. Quality numbers may be in spreadsheets. | Mixed |
| Morning of arrival | Printing or opening the patient list, the OR schedule, 12 months of minutes, drill matrix, license file | Patient list and OR schedule are in the EHR if someone can pull them fast | The patient list is PHI |
| During tracers | Staff leave the bedside to answer. Chart is opened patient by patient. | Oracle Health chart, medication cabinet transactions, device data | PHI |
| Competence and credentialing sessions | Pulling files for the people the surveyor names | SnapSkill completions; Dossier if that is the competency system; medical staff files unknown | Workforce, plus peer-review limits on medical staff files |
| Life safety days | Walking, and producing inspection records that are often PDFs from vendors | Sometimes a CMMS, often a binder. Unknown here | Usually not PHI |
| 60 days after | Writing the ESC: who, what, date, sustainment plan, and for higher-risk items a leadership and preventive-analysis narrative | The finding list arrives as a report on the extranet, not as an API into the hospital | The narrative can include PHI if someone pastes a case |

The product opportunity is not "replace the surveyor." Surveyors will still walk and interview. The opportunity is that the morning pile and the 60-day evidence packet are assembled from systems continuously, with an owner and a date on every EP, so arrival is a query instead of a search.

## What this packet cannot say about the last survey

Open, all of these:

- Date, length, and team size of the recent survey
- Whether findings were standard-level, condition-level, or an immediate threat
- Which chapters were cited
- Whether they are in a follow-up or a clean window
- Which ORYX / performance-measurement vendor they use now (`APR.04.01.01` required one in the 2015 model)

Those facts live with the accreditation lead and on Joint Commission Connect, not in this folder.
